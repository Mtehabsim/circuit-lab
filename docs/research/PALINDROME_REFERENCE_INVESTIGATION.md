# The Palindrome Machine: reference investigation

This is a reproducible, bounded investigation of the independently trained checkpoint at `backend/circuit_api/checkpoints/palindrome-machine.pt`. It records measured evidence rather than assigning universal roles to components. The model is not the model used in ARENA's published palindrome exercise, and its learned computation must be investigated on its own terms.

## Question and model

The task is binary classification: given a sequence over `A`–`H`, predict whether it reads identically in reverse. The checkpoint was trained from generated examples with seed 1729, 5,000 training examples, 45 epochs, AdamW (learning rate 0.003, weight decay 0.01), and gradient clipping at 1.0. The full configuration and data seeds are recorded beside the checkpoint in `palindrome-machine.provenance.json`.

The decoder has three causal-attention layers, four heads per layer, width 48, learned absolute positions, pre-layer-normalization, GELU MLPs of width 96, and a two-class readout at EOS. Symbols are embedded with BOS/EOS markers. No circuit is manually inserted. The checkpoint is one selected seed, not evidence about all models trained on this task.

## 1. Characterize behavior before interpreting it

The recorded generated validation accuracy is 98.9%; the fixed public set is 100% and the fixed hidden set is 94/96 (97.9%). These are finite samples, not a guarantee of algorithmic correctness. On independent 1,500-example probes generated by `palindrome_experiments.py`, accuracy was:

| Probe distribution | Accuracy |
|---|---:|
| IID generated task examples | 99.1% |
| Repeated two-symbol alphabet with mixed labels | 92.9% |
| Exactly one mirrored mismatch | 99.8% |
| Exactly two mirrored mismatches | 95.2% |
| Exactly three mirrored mismatches | 99.5% |

The repeated-symbol result is a useful warning: strong IID accuracy does not imply uniform performance over every sequence distribution. A separate 200-example length 13–16 probe is below 70% (approximately chance); those lengths exceed the training range and should be described as out of distribution. The challenge does not use them as ordinary hidden generalization cases.

The high single-mismatch score supports the proposition that the model can often detect a disagreement at any one mirrored pair. It does not establish that it explicitly compares every pair. The small controlled samples and multiple-mismatch variation leave room for length- and pattern-dependent strategies.

## 2. Attention and QK evidence

The reproducible probe creates 600 positive strings, changes one character on the right half, and compares the query at the changed character with the key at its mirrored position in the matched clean/corrupted pair. For L0H0, the mean pre-softmax QK score at that mirrored key changes from 8.92 to -0.34; the mean attention weight changes from 0.518 to 0.090. The mirrored-key logit margin over the strongest other visible content key changes from +5.30 to -3.05. L0H1 shows a similar direction (QK 3.00 to -2.20; attention 0.323 to 0.0059; margin +0.77 to -3.53).

This is evidence that these heads' *early attention selection is sensitive to token agreement at paired positions* on this probe. It is not a complete QK explanation: the measured Q and K already include the residual stream and LayerNorm, which mix token and positional information. It also does not show that attention alone computes the final label. L0H2 behaves differently in the same probe: its mirrored-pair QK rises from -4.09 to +2.97 after corruption, while the mirror-vs-other margin remains negative. This is a concrete counterexample to treating every visually responsive head as a positive “matcher.”

The plot generated by the reproducibility script summarizes mirrored-position attention for L0H0/L0H1 by label. Attention weights are observational evidence. The milestone asks learners to implement a tensor statistic and compare cases; it explicitly does not treat a matching plot as causal proof.

## 3. OV and residual evidence

For a head, the local output calculation is `pattern @ V @ W_O`. CircuitLab's mechanism milestone asks learners to implement scaled `Q @ K.T` and this OV operation from raw tensors, then independently checks both on hidden model activations and fresh synthetic tensors. This verifies that the learner can reconstruct the local attention computation rather than call a convenience function. By itself, the generic formula does not establish what semantic information a particular trained head carries.

The head output is added to the residual stream, followed by the MLP update. Later LayerNorms, attention, MLP nonlinearities, and the final unembedding mean that projecting one local output onto the final class direction is not an exact full-model logit attribution. The current reference investigation therefore does not label a local class-direction projection as a complete OV explanation. A stronger investigation would quantify the projection and then validate its prediction by component/path intervention through the actual downstream network.

## 4. Causal interventions and interactions

On the fixed 96-example hidden set, independent zero ablation gives:

| Intervention | Accuracy after ablation | Accuracy change from 97.9% baseline |
|---|---:|---:|
| L0H0 | 61.5% | -36.5 points |
| L0H1 | 68.8% | -29.2 points |
| L0H0 + L0H1 | 54.2% | -43.8 points |
| MLP0 | 75.0% | -22.9 points |
| MLP1 | 97.9% | 0 points |
| MLP2 | 89.6% | -8.3 points |

Removing L0H0 and L0H1 together has a smaller accuracy effect than adding the two separate drops, consistent with overlapping contributions or nonlinear interaction. Accuracy is a coarse statistic, so the reference script also computes margin-level interaction probes; those too depend on the probe distribution and do not identify a unique decomposition. MLP0's substantial ablation effect makes an attention-only account incomplete. These interventions establish causal effects under this task distribution, not what each component computes.

For each hidden example the script defines a difference-in-differences on the signed class margin: `margin(both ablated) - margin(left only) - margin(right only) + margin(baseline)`. Mean absolute interactions were 10.49 logits for L0H0×L0H1, 6.39 for L0H0×MLP0, and 6.48 for L0H1×MLP0 (96 examples each). These large non-additivities show that the effect of removing one component depends on the state of another under this intervention. They are not proof of a direct communication edge or a complete circuit; ablation changes downstream states and the classifier is nonlinear.

## 5. Matched patching: contribution versus decision localization

The script forms 80 matched pairs where the model predicts a palindrome correctly, then changes one right-side token so it predicts a non-palindrome. For each pair it copies a clean component output into the corrupted run at the changed position, and compares with a control patch at the mirrored earlier position. Mean absolute movement in the final class margin was 10.01 logits for L0H0 at the changed position and 0.00 for the paired control; L0H1 was 9.44 versus 0.00; MLP0 was 9.99 versus 0.00. Yet the target-site patches restored the positive classification on 0%, 3.75%, and 0% of pairs respectively.

This result is informative precisely because a large causal margin shift is not the same as restoring the decision. These component outputs affect downstream evidence at the changed location, but a one-site patch does not usually erase the computation of the corruption. Nor does a contrast with this particular earlier-position control prove a unique pathway: the patch is an activation replacement that can be out of distribution for the corrupted downstream state.

Patching the entire EOS residual after layer 0 restores a positive margin on 22.5% of these pairs; after layer 1, 80%; and after layer 2, 100%. The last operation replaces the state immediately before the classifier with a representation already containing the computed decision. It supports *decision localization* only. CircuitLab gives this no credit toward the upstream information-flow milestone.

## 6. Best-supported account and alternatives

The current evidence supports a cautious account: early attention heads L0H0 and L0H1 respond to agreement between some mirrored token positions; their outputs causally influence classification; MLP0 also contributes substantially; and later residual states increasingly contain a representation sufficient for the binary readout. The pairwise patch control suggests position-specific component effects in the tested corruption setup. The joint ablation and model's multiple layers leave open whether equality evidence is accumulated through multiple interacting or partially redundant routes.

An alternative is that early components detect a mixture of symbol/position conjunctions and that later MLP/residual computations combine these signals, without implementing a clean reusable equality comparator for every pair. The restricted-alphabet degradation and the lack of consistent patch restoration leave this alternative viable. We have not yet decomposed Q/K into token-versus-position terms, established a full OV-to-downstream causal path, performed path patching, or shown sufficiency of a minimal circuit. This is not a complete reverse-engineering of the checkpoint.

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

```sh
cd backend
uv run python ../docs/research/palindrome_experiments.py
```

The script regenerates `docs/research/generated/palindrome_measurements.json` and `layer0_mirrored_attention.png` from the bundled checkpoint and fixed probe seeds. It reports the behavior probes, QK/attention paired measurements, individual and joint ablations, matched component/control patches, and final-residual patch rates. The exact generated numbers are estimates over those specified finite probes; rerunning with changed seeds is a useful robustness check.

## What the mission verifies

The staged grader verifies: (1) a learner-written length-stratified behavior metric; (2) a learner-written attention statistic on hidden tensors; (3) recorded and independently replayed causal ablations of a small candidate set; (4) learner-written QK/OV tensor calculations; (5) learner-written component patch predictions checked against independent matched target/control replay; and (6) a predicted comparison against an alternative component on additional hidden pairs. This is evidence of transferable experimental skills and particular quantitative claims. The score does not certify a unique natural-language explanation or a complete circuit.

## Research takeaways

- High task accuracy is not evidence that the model implements the human algorithm in the expected way.
- A useful head sweep narrows the search, but ablation establishes causal contribution rather than computation.
- QK behavior and OV output are distinct questions; attention alone is not an explanation.
- MLP ablation matters here, so a head-only story is incomplete.
- Patching a late state can localize a decision after computation; it cannot explain how the decision was produced.
- Matched examples, controls, predictions, and out-of-distribution tests make claims sharper, while still leaving mechanistic uncertainty.
